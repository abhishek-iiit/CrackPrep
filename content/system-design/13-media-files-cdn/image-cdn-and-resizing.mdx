## The phone does not want your 12 MP original

A client upload may be a 4–12 megapixel JPEG of several megabytes. A feed thumbnail needs a few kilobytes at 200 px. Shipping the original everywhere wastes mobile bandwidth, burns client decode time, and multiplies origin egress. Generating every width×format variant at upload time wastes storage and forces a combinatorial explosion (width × crop × format × DPR).

An **image CDN** (or image-aware origin shield) sits in the middle: store one or a few masters, accept requests that name the desired transformation, produce the derivative on first miss, and cache it at the edge. The product talks in logical URLs; the edge talks in bytes.

## Request shape

Common patterns:

- **Path-based:** `/img/\{id\}/w400/h300/fit-cover.webp`
- **Query-based:** `/img/\{id\}?w=400&h=300&fit=cover&fm=webp`
- **Preset-based:** `/img/\{id\}/thumb` where `thumb` is a server-defined recipe (safest against parameter abuse)

Presets are the usual production preference for user-generated content: clients cannot ask for `w=8000` and turn your CPU into a free render farm. Open parameter spaces need hard caps, allowlists, and signed URLs.

## Pipeline on a cache miss

<Steps>
  <Step title="Edge receives the derivative URL">
    CDN checks whether this exact variant is cached. Hit → serve. Miss → continue.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Authorize if required">
    Private images need signed URLs or token validation at the edge/origin. Public avatars may skip this.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fetch the master from object storage">
    Preferably from a nearby origin shield or regional cache of masters so every POP does not pull from a single distant bucket.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Decode, transform, encode">
    Resize, crop, strip EXIF (privacy), convert to WebP/AVIF/JPEG based on `Accept` or an explicit `fm`. Cap pixel dimensions and output bytes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Store the derivative and serve">
    Write through to edge cache (and optionally a persistent derivative bucket). Set long `Cache-Control` for immutable URLs; short or versioned for editable images.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Immutability is a lever: if the URL includes a content hash or version (`v=3`), you can cache for a year and purge by changing the version in your HTML/API. If the URL is stable and the bytes change, you need explicit CDN purge on every edit — slower and easier to get wrong.

## Eager variants vs on-demand

| Strategy | When it fits | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| On-demand at edge | Many possible sizes, long-tail images rarely viewed | First-hit latency; CPU on miss |
| Eager at upload (fixed presets) | Few presets, every image is viewed hot (avatars) | Storage × preset count; upload-path latency or queue |
| Hybrid | Eager thumbs + on-demand large/hero | Best common compromise |

Avatars and feed thumbs often eager-generate two or three sizes (13.03). Product photography libraries lean on-demand with presets.

## Formats and responsive delivery

Modern stacks negotiate AVIF/WebP with JPEG fallback. The CDN can vary on `Accept` — but then the cache key must include that variance, or you will serve WebP to a client that asked for JPEG. Explicit `fm=` in the URL makes cache keys obvious at the cost of smarter clients or server-side user-agent logic in HTML generation (`<picture>` sources).

Retina / DPR: either accept a `dpr=2` parameter inside a capped preset or generate 2× widths in the preset list. Unbounded DPR is another CPU abuse vector.

## Where the bytes live

Masters stay in a private (or tightly ACL'd) bucket. Derivatives may live only on the CDN cache, or also be written through to a derivatives bucket for warmer long-tail hits after edge eviction. Write-through costs storage; cache-only costs more origin CPU on the next miss. Hot avatars and catalog heroes almost always deserve write-through or eager publish. Long-tail UGC often accepts cache-only with an origin shield so a viral miss collapses to one resize, not one per POP.

## Failure modes

- **Origin stampede** on a popular new image before the edge is warm — use origin shield and singleflight / request collapsing.
- **Uncapped transforms** — attacker-forced 8K AVIF encodes; allowlist presets.
- **EXIF orientation and GPS** — strip location metadata from user photos before public serve.
- **Animated GIF/WebP** — resize paths that only handle stills will break stickers; detect and special-case.
- **Cache poisoning via unsigned open params** — sign transformation URLs when params are client-influenced.

<Callout type="gotcha">
"We purge the CDN on every profile photo change" without changing the URL is how half the internet still sees the old avatar. Prefer versioned or hash-based URLs so the HTML reference moves forward and old edge entries simply expire unused.
</Callout>

## In an interview

Store masters in object storage (13.01), serve via CDN with **preset** transforms, cache derivatives at the edge, version URLs on change. Mention EXIF stripping and stampede control. Distinguish eager avatar thumbs from on-demand catalog images. Link forward to video: images are resize-cache; video needs a transcoding pipeline (13.05) and ABR (13.06).
