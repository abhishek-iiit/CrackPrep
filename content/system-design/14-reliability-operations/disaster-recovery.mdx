## A second region that has never received traffic is a hope, not a plan

Disaster recovery (DR) is the capability to continue or restore service after a large-scale failure — an availability zone gone dark, a region offline, a provider control-plane event, a corrupted primary that replication cheerfully copied everywhere. Backups (14.07, 14.08) recover *data*; DR recovers *service*, which also needs compute, DNS, secrets, queues, and people who know the order of operations.

The expensive mistake is buying multi-region replication and never failing over until a real disaster, when DNS TTLs, cold caches, license servers, and forgotten stateful workers all surface at once.

## Topologies and what they buy

| Topology | Data posture | Failover speed | Cost / complexity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Backup/restore to another region | Cold; RPO tied to backup | Hours+ | Lowest steady cost |
| Pilot light | Minimal core in DR region; data replicated | Tens of minutes–hours after scale-out | Low–medium |
| Warm standby | Scaled-down stack taking replicated data | Minutes | Medium |
| Hot standby / active-passive | Full capacity ready; traffic flipped | Minutes or less | High |
| Active-active | Serves traffic in multiple regions | Fail *away* from damaged region | Highest; conflict and latency design required |

Active-active is not "warm standby with a marketing name." It demands session affinity or global data design, conflict rules, and latency budgets between regions. Most products that need DR do not need active-active on day one; they need a warm standby with a rehearsed flip.

## Failover is a product decision

<Steps>
  <Step title="Define the disaster declaration">
    Who can declare, on what signals (multi-AZ failure, provider status, data corruption), and what is *not* a DR event (single-node failure the HA layer handles).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Promote data plane">
    Promote a replica carefully — ensure replicas were not poisoned by replicated bad writes if the disaster is logical corruption. Sometimes the answer is PITR in-region, not regional failover.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Point traffic">
    DNS, anycast, or global load balancer shifts. Account for TTL and client sticky behavior; lower TTLs before you need them.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Bring dependent systems">
    Queues, object storage failover, feature flag defaults, signing keys for media (13.07), cron leaders — the shadow inventory that breaks first-time failovers.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Operate degraded and communicate">
    Some features may stay off. Status page and IC roles (14.03) apply; DR is an incident with a bigger blast radius.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="gotcha">
Asynchronous cross-region replication means failover can lose the last few seconds or minutes of writes — that loss *is* your RPO. Pretending async replication gives RPO zero sets false expectations; sync cross-region commits buy lower RPO at a latency tax on every write.
</Callout>

## Corruption versus outage

Regional outage: promote the other region's replica. Logical corruption or ransomware that replicated everywhere: promoting the replica makes things worse. Keep delayed replicas or immutable point-in-time backups that are not immediately overwritten by continuous replication. DR runbooks must branch on "region dead" versus "data poisoned."

## Drills beat documents

Run scheduled failover games: flip staging globally, or fail a non-critical region in production during a maintenance window. Measure actual RTO. Fix automation gaps (infrastructure as code for the DR region, sealed secrets, capacity quotas). Update runbooks where humans stumbled. A DR plan last edited two years ago is fiction.

Dependencies on a single global control plane (one identity provider, one DNS host) need their own DR story or they become the hidden SPOF inside your multi-region diagram.

## In an interview

Frame DR as recovering *service* in another failure domain with an explicit RPO/RTO and a tested failover runbook. Name the topology you choose and why (usually warm standby before active-active). Call out async replication data loss and the corruption-versus-outage branch. "We replicate to another region" without drills and DNS/TTL planning is the answer interviewers punish.
