## Zero results is often a spelling problem wearing a retrieval costume

The inverted index only knows terms it was built with. A query token one edit off a popular term — `"nikke shoes"`, `"iphoen case"` — can miss every posting list that would have made a good result page. Users rarely blame their typing; they blame search. Spell correction exists to close that gap inside the query understanding pipeline (09.07 Query Understanding Pipeline), after tokenization and before expansion and retrieval.

The design tension is not "can we find a closer dictionary word." It is **when to trust a rewrite**. Silent correction of `"ruby"` to `"rubi"` in a jewelry context, or of a rare brand into a common competitor, destroys precision. Showing "Did you mean …?" preserves agency; auto-correcting only when confidence is high preserves recall without training users that the box cannot be trusted.

## Mechanisms that are good enough under a latency budget

Correction has to run on the query path. Exotic models lose to simple methods that hit a vocabulary sized to your corpus and logs.

| Approach | Idea | Strength | Weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Edit distance to lexicon | Generate candidates within 1–2 Damerau-Levenshtein edits; rank by frequency | Predictable, language-light | Costly if naively scanned; weak on phonetic errors |
| Symmetric delete / BK-trees / tries | Index deletes of dictionary words for fast candidate gen | Fast candidate retrieval | Memory and build complexity |
| Phonetic codes (Soundex, Metaphone) | Match similarly pronounced tokens | Catches "smith"/"smyth"-class errors | Coarse; many false friends |
| Log-trained noisy channel | P(intended) × P(typed \| intended) from reformulations | Reflects real user fixes | Needs volume; cold start for new terms |
| Neural correctors | Sequence models | Strong on hard cases | Latency, ops cost, harder to explain |

Production systems often **cascade**: if the raw query already retrieves well (enough hits, healthy click model), skip correction. If not, generate candidates from a dictionary built from index terms plus head queries, score with edit distance and frequency, and either suggest or rewrite.

<Callout type="gotcha">
Dictionary pollution is the quiet failure. If yesterday's misspellings and bot queries enter the lexicon as first-class terms, the corrector learns to "fix" good queries into garbage, or to treat typos as authoritative. Build the lexicon from index vocabulary and vetted head queries; put a frequency floor on anything promoted from raw logs.
</Callout>

## Suggest versus auto-rewrite

Two UX contracts, two risk profiles:

- **Did you mean** — keep the original retrieval (even if thin), show a prominent alternative. User opts in. Safer for ambiguous tokens and brands.
- **Auto-correct / silent rewrite** — replace the query before retrieval when confidence is high: very low original hit count, one dominant candidate, small edit distance, candidate is much more frequent. Show a "showing results for X — search instead for Y" affordance so the user can undo.

Confidence should be a real threshold you can tune against judged sets or online metrics (09.09 Search Evaluation Metrics), not an engineer’s gut feel on a whiteboard. Weight corrected terms carefully when you still mix original and corrected tokens — the pipeline lesson still holds: a guess is not equal to what the user typed.

For multi-term queries, correct token-by-token with a cap on total edits, and prefer corrections that improve the whole query’s hit rate rather than greedily "fixing" every token. Proper nouns and SKUs often belong on an allowlist that refuses correction.

## In an interview

Introduce correction as a confidence-gated stage in query understanding: "I only rewrite when the raw query is likely to fail and a much more frequent near-neighbor exists; otherwise I suggest." Walk through candidate generation (edits against a lexicon), ranking (frequency × closeness), and the suggest-versus-rewrite UX. Expect "what about brands?" — allowlists and never correcting high-confidence proper nouns. Name dictionary pollution before you are asked why correction got worse after a traffic spike from bots.
