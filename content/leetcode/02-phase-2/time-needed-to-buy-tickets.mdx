## Simulating every second works — but you can count rounds

People stand in a queue; each second the person at the front buys one ticket and goes to the back if they still need more. Given ticket counts and index `k`, return how many seconds until person `k` finishes (buys their last ticket).

Naive simulation runs a loop every second, decrementing the front — O(total tickets), which can reach 100 × 100 = 10⁴ here, acceptable but unnecessary. Person `k` buys once per full queue rotation until their count hits zero, plus partial rotations at the end.

## Count full rounds plus the tail

Person `k` participates in every second until their last ticket. In each full pass through the queue, they buy exactly once — that costs `n` seconds per round. After `tickets[k] - 1` full rounds they have one ticket left. For rounds before they finish, everyone ahead with tickets remaining also buys once per round.

Direct formula: sum over all `i` of `min(tickets[i], tickets[k])` when `i < k`, plus `min(tickets[i], tickets[k] - 1)` when `i > k`, plus `tickets[k]` for person k themselves — or simulate with a queue in one pass.

<Steps>
<Step title="For each person i, count how many times they buy before k finishes">
  If i ≤ k: they buy min(tickets[i], tickets[k]) times. If i > k: min(tickets[i], tickets[k] - 1).
</Step>
<Step title="Sum all purchases — each purchase is one second">
  Person k always contributes tickets[k] seconds; others contribute less once k is on their last ticket.
</Step>
<Step title="Alternatively, simulate with a queue of (index, remaining) pairs">
  Stop when person k's remaining hits 0; count seconds elapsed.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Solution

```ts
function timeRequiredToBuy(tickets: number[], k: number): number {
  let seconds = 0;

  for (let i = 0; i < tickets.length; i++) {
    if (i <= k) {
      seconds += Math.min(tickets[i], tickets[k]);
    } else {
      seconds += Math.min(tickets[i], tickets[k] - 1);
    }
  }

  return seconds;
}
```

Time **O(n)**. Space **O(1)**.

## In an interview

Restate: `[2, 3, 2]`, k = 2 → 6 seconds. Confirm one ticket per second, front person buys then re-queues if more tickets remain, stop when person k buys their last.

Say out loud that simulating every second is O(total tickets) but person k's finish time depends only on how many times each person buys before k's last purchase — O(n) counting. Mention the asymmetry: people after k do not get a turn during k's final ticket.

Follow-ups: priority queue variant, or multiple ticket windows. The bug interviewers watch for is using `tickets[k]` for everyone instead of `tickets[k] - 1` for indices after k.
